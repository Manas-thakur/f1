## 4. The elevation defect and what was done about it

### 4.1 The defect

Suzuka's raw z channel carries isolated single-sample spikes. Measured on the
cleaned samples at the inferred scale, 8 samples across the 12 retained laps
break the plausible elevation budget on both sides at once, at two recurring
places in the lap (t ≈ 39 s and t ≈ 77 s after the timing line, in laps 12, 13,
14 ×2, 15 ×2, 16 and 18).
The signature is unambiguous:

```
lap 14  t=39.38 s  g_in=+0.767  g_out=-0.917   z[..] = -4.5 -4.6 -4.6 +3.1 -4.6 -4.6 -4.6
lap 18  t=39.27 s  g_in=+0.748  g_out=-1.130   z[..] = -4.3 -4.4 -4.4 +3.8 -4.4 -4.4 -4.4
```

The road would have to rise 7.7 m over 10 m and fall 7.7 m over 8 m, inside one
sampling interval. The largest raw per-segment gradient on suzuka was 1.130
(113 %) and the implied vertical speed reached 58.6 m/s.

Why it survived to `grade_rad`: the aggregation takes the *median* elevation per
5 m arc bin, but OpenF1 samples at ~3.7 Hz, i.e. roughly one sample per 16 m of
track at racing speed. Across 12 laps a 5 m bin holds about four samples, so one
or two spikes in the same bin win the median. Grade then comes from the spline
whose bandwidth was chosen entirely on *horizontal* held-out residual. Grade
enters the engine's force balance as `m g sin(grade)`
(`simulation/physics.py`, `evaluation/independent_ledger.py:106`), so a 91 %
gradient is fake energy demand — exactly what `TRACK_DATA_PIPELINE.md` forbids
for curvature.

Monza (max 0.036 raw, 0.028 smoothed) and spa (0.189 raw, 0.153 smoothed, over a
102.4 m z span — Raidillon) are clean, and their spike count is zero, which is
the evidence that the horizontal pipeline was never the problem.

### 4.2 Rejection threshold, derived not tuned

`reject_z_outliers` (in `compile.py`). Two declared physical constants:

* `ROAD_GRADE_CEILING = 0.25`. The steepest gradients on the calendar are
  Raidillon (~18 %) and the climb to Austin's turn 1 (~12 %); no Grand Prix
  circuit sustains 25 %. It is a statement about roads, and it is the same value
  the independent validator applies (`validate.GRADE_BOUND`), reached
  independently rather than copied to make a circuit pass.
* `VERTICAL_SPEED_CEILING_MPS = SPEED_CEILING_MPS * ROAD_GRADE_CEILING = 27.5`
  m/s — derived from the existing speed ceiling, not chosen.

The per-interval budget is `|dz| ≤ ROAD_GRADE_CEILING * horizontal displacement
+ one z quantum`, which is the vertical-speed limit expressed at whatever cadence
the feed happens to deliver (`|dz/dt| ≤ grade_ceiling * v_horizontal`). The z
quantum is one raw coordinate unit in metres (0.1 m here) because the source
publishes integers; without it, a sample taken while the car is nearly
stationary in a hairpin fails a ratio test purely on quantisation.

A sample is rejected only when it breaks the budget **on both sides with
opposite signs** — an elevation excursion that reverses inside one sampling
interval, which a static surface cannot produce. A one-sided or same-sign excess
is a candidate genuine slope: it is counted as `steep_segments_retained` and
kept, never deleted. That distinction matters in practice — Monaco has one
0.884 one-sided event (0.5 m of descent over 0.57 m of horizontal travel, i.e.
at 2.4 m/s: a near-standstill, where the horizontal displacement is smaller than
the position noise and the ratio is not a trustworthy gradient) and suzuka keeps
3 after rejection; none of them is silently removed, and all of them survive
into the reported numbers.

A rejected sample keeps its x and y; only its z is replaced, by linear
interpolation in horizontal arc length between the nearest trusted samples —
the same continuity assumption the spline makes everywhere else. Nothing is
zeroed: a zero grade is a claim about the road, an interpolated one is a
labelled estimate. Rejection is capped at 5 % of a lap's samples and 4 passes;
*isolated* means sparse, and if more than that breaks the budget the channel is
not repairable by outlier rejection at all — the cap trips, `rejection_cap_reached`
is recorded, and the plausibility check in 4.4 decides.

Recorded per lap in `compile_report.json` under `laps[].elevation_cleaning`
(samples, `rejected_spikes`, `rejected_fraction`, `steep_segments_retained`,
passes, cap flag, max segment grade and vertical speed before/after, the
thresholds and the rule text) and aggregated under
`geometry.elevation.rejected_z_samples`.

### 4.3 The elevation channel has its own smoothing selection

`choose_elevation_smoothing`. The elevation channel is fitted on the same
aggregated points and the same knots as the horizontal channel but with its own
penalty weight; `resample_by_arclength(tck, z_tck=...)` then takes x, y, yaw and
curvature from the horizontal fit and z and grade from the elevation fit, and
checks that the two splines really do share knots rather than assuming it.

The bandwidth is chosen on **vertical** evidence: each fold aggregates the
training laps, fits a candidate bandwidth and measures the residual of the
held-out laps' z samples at their projected arc length; the one-standard-error
rule takes the smoothest bandwidth the data cannot distinguish from the best.
The candidate list extends past the horizontal one (to 320 m) because a road's
elevation is a smoother field than a racing line's lateral scatter; the held-out
vertical residual still bounds it from above, exactly as a chicane bounds the
horizontal choice. **The selection never looks at the resulting grade**, so it
cannot be tuned to satisfy the bound — but the grade each candidate implies is
recorded next to it, so the consequence is visible. Suzuka's table:

| z bandwidth (m) | held-out vertical RMS (m) | max &#124;grade&#124; |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | 0.1823 | 0.1868 |
| 7.5 | **0.1749** (best) | 0.1175 |
| 10 | 0.2065 | 0.0807 |
| **15 (chosen)** | 0.1868 | **0.0806** |
| 30 | 0.2806 | 0.0772 |
| 320 | 3.7428 | 0.0285 |

Suzuka is the only circuit where the vertical hold-out prefers a wider bandwidth
than the horizontal one (15 m against 5 m). On the other five the vertical
residual is minimised at 5 m and the one-SE rule keeps 5 m, so their z channel is
bit-identical to what a single shared bandwidth would have produced. Both
mechanisms contributed at suzuka: rejection alone brings the smoothed maximum to
0.1868, and the vertical bandwidth then reaches 0.0806.

### 4.4 A plausibility check that declares elevation unavailable

`assess_elevation` recomputes max and p99 &#124;grade&#124; on the final 1 m grid. If any
sample still exceeds `ROAD_GRADE_CEILING`, the profile is not a measurement of a
road, and `build_centreline` publishes the elevation channel as `nan` — never as
zeros — with `elevation_channel_unavailable` in `flags`, a named `reason` in
`geometry.elevation` and `unavailable.elevation`, and a note in the package's
`ValidationReport`.

**`CompiledCentreline` refuses that today** (`package.py`: "z contains a
non-finite value"), so the null channel cannot be stored. That is seam request
(a) in section 8. Until it is granted, the fallback keeps the measured — and
implausible — z and grade *unaltered*, adds
`elevation_null_channel_not_storable` to `flags` and writes two notes saying the
arrays are not a road profile and must not be read as one. The independent
validator's `grade_bounded` check then fails on them and the circuit is
`rejected`: a status the validator refuses, which is what the brief asked for in
place of a fabricated profile. Neither branch ships an invented number.

Verified by running both branches directly (not as a committed test — see
section 9):

```
spiked lap: segment grade 2.718 -> 0.0267 after rejection, 1 sample rejected,
            vertical speed 149.5 -> 1.5 m/s
smoothed max |grade| with the spike 1.2321 (ceiling 0.25) -> after rejection 0.0267
available=False
reason=max |grade| 0.9000 exceeds the declared ceiling 0.25 after rejecting 3 of 400
       raw z samples as isolated spikes and choosing the elevation bandwidth (15 m) on
       held-out vertical residual; the rejection cap was reached, so the z channel is
       not repairable by outlier rejection at all
null channel blocked by: CompiledCentreline refuses a null elevation channel: z contains
       a non-finite value
arrays kept the measured grade (not zeros): max |grade| 0.900, any zero grade sample: False
```

On real data the same mechanism took suzuka from a raw per-segment gradient of
1.130 to a published maximum of 0.0806 while keeping the 40.3 m z span, which is
the physically right span for Suzuka.

One bookkeeping caveat: `validate_track` replaces the package's
`ValidationReport.notes` with its own evidence notes, so the compiler's
elevation note survives in `package.json` only until the circuit is validated.
The durable record is `compile_report.json` — `flags`, `geometry.elevation`
(the whole `ElevationChannel`, including `available`, `reason`, the bandwidth,
the rejected-sample count and `nan_emission_blocked_by`) and
`unavailable.elevation` — plus `laps[].elevation_cleaning` per lap. If you want
the declaration to survive in the validated package, it needs a field rather
than a note; that is part of seam request (a).

## 5. Suzuka does not reach `geometry_validated`, and elevation is not why

Every geometry check now passes except one, and it is not a geometry defect:

```
grade_bounded: max |grade| = 0.0806 (bound 0.25)            pass
direction: declared direction is mixed; signed area cannot confirm it   unknown
status discovered: geometry checks not passed: direction
```

Suzuka crosses over itself, so its **net turning is +0.000 rad (0.000
revolutions)** while its signed area is −123 288 m². `loop_direction` therefore
reports `mixed`, which is the honest answer to the question the function asks;
the manifest declares `mixed` too, and the two agree. But the validator's
`direction` check can only confirm a package that declares clockwise or
counterclockwise, so it returns `unknown`, and `_derive_status` caps any circuit
with a non-`pass` geometry check at `discovered`.

I did not "fix" this by declaring suzuka clockwise: the signed area is dominated
by the larger lobe and would give clockwise, but that would be the compiler
asserting a fact its own test says it cannot establish. It is seam request (b)
in section 8.

## 6. Task 2 — the 1.3–1.8 % length deficit at monaco, mexico-city and singapore

`length_tolerance_fraction` was **not** touched (still 1 % everywhere, D-12).
This section is investigation only.

### 6.1 It is not lap selection

Per-lap arc length of the 12 retained laps (own periodic fit, 3-D):

| circuit | mean | sd | min | max | range | deficit vs official |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| monaco | 3275.3 m | 0.26 m | 3274.7 | 3275.7 | 1.0 m | **63.2 m** |
| singapore | 4862.5 m | 0.48 m | 4861.3 | 4862.9 | 1.6 m | **64.7 m** |
| mexico-city | 4247.9 m | 8.74 m | 4244.2 | 4275.6 | 31.4 m | **58.8 m** |
| monza | 5761.0 m | 0.32 m | 5760.4 | 5761.4 | 1.0 m | 32.1 m |
| spa | 6958.1 m | 0.55 m | 6957.1 | 6958.5 | 1.5 m | 45.9 m |
| suzuka | 5779.1 m | 1.68 m | 5776.0 | 5782.1 | 6.1 m | 28.2 m |

The deficit is 243 times the lap-to-lap standard deviation at monaco, 135 times
at singapore and 7 times at mexico-city (whose spread is inflated by a single
anomalous lap). Every retained lap is short by essentially the same amount, so no
choice among these laps moves the result by more than a metre or two at monaco
and singapore. The selection rule also biases the wrong way for this
explanation: laps are picked closest to the *session median* duration, i.e.
mid-race laps in traffic and fuel-saving, which run *wider*, not tighter, than a
qualifying lap. Mexico-city is the one circuit with a genuinely anomalous lap
(one lap 31 m longer than the shortest, lap-to-lap sd 8.7 m); dropping it would
lengthen the median line by well under 10 m, not 59 m.

### 6.2 It is not the timing line, the closure, or the smoothing

* Timing-line alignment rotates `s` and is asserted not to change length;
  `length_before_alignment_m == length_after_alignment_m` in every report, and
  the median crossing sits 0.001–0.03 m from the fitted line.
* Closure error is 1.5e-06–2.9e-04 m — the loop closes to well under a
  millimetre, so no length is lost at the seam.
* Smoothing removes almost nothing: the raw cleaned polyline and the fitted
  spline agree to within ~2 m (monaco raw 3273.3 m vs spline 3275.3 m; the
  spline is *longer*, because the raw closing chord cuts the corner). At the
  bandwidth actually chosen (5 m) the fit is not shortening the path.

### 6.3 It is not a coordinate-scale error

The implied scale per circuit is 0.10058 (monza), 0.10062 (suzuka), 0.10081
(spa), 0.10136 (singapore), 0.10141 (mexico-city), 0.10197 (monaco) m/unit
against the 1 dm hypothesis. A wrong unit would be one constant factor and would
put every circuit off by the same fraction. These span 0.58 %–1.97 %, and their
order is the order of the deficits, so the deficit is a property of the *driven
line*, present in the raw integers before any fitting, not of the frame.

### 6.4 It is quantitatively consistent with a driven line cutting apexes

Cutting a corner of turn angle Δθ by w metres shortens the path by w·Δθ, so to
first order `deficit ≈ w · ∮|κ| ds` with one parameter, the effective apex inset
w. Measuring `∮|κ| ds` on each compiled line:

| circuit | ∮&#124;κ&#124;ds | turning density | deficit | deficit % | implied inset w |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| monza | 16.38 rad | 2.83 rad/km | 32.1 m | 0.554 % | 1.96 m |
| spa | 29.73 rad | 4.24 rad/km | 45.9 m | 0.655 % | 1.54 m |
| suzuka | 30.38 rad | 5.23 rad/km | 28.2 m | 0.486 % | 0.93 m |
| monaco | 31.17 rad | 9.34 rad/km | 63.2 m | 1.894 % | 2.03 m |
| singapore | 27.51 rad | 5.58 rad/km | 64.7 m | 1.313 % | 2.35 m |
| mexico-city | 24.26 rad | 5.64 rad/km | 58.8 m | 1.366 % | 2.42 m |

One inset of **1.87 m ± 0.56 m** explains all six deficits, and the *fractional*
deficit correlates with turning density at **r = 0.859**. Monaco is the extreme
of the turning-density axis (9.34 rad/km, 19 corners in 3.3 km) and the extreme
of the deficit, exactly as the model predicts. An inset of ~2 m is what a 2 m
wide car on a 10–12 m road can take at an apex.

### 6.5 Direct measurement: a wide line versus a tight line

I measured, not assumed, how much arc length lateral freedom is worth. Around
each compiled line I relaxed a minimum-length closed path constrained to stay
within ±w (elastic band, motion along the local normal only), and separately
offset the line by w towards the outside of every corner:

| circuit | tight line, ±1 m | ±2 m | ±3 m | ±5 m | wide line, +2 m outward | +5 m outward |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| monaco | −0.95 % | **−1.74 %** | −2.45 % | −3.71 % | +5.33 % | +14.88 % |
| singapore | −0.52 % | −1.01 % | −1.47 % | −2.29 % | +7.83 % | +22.26 % |
| mexico-city | −0.52 % | −1.00 % | −1.45 % | −2.27 % | +10.12 % | +28.89 % |
| monza | −0.26 % | −0.49 % | −0.69 % | −1.01 % | +12.32 % | +35.47 % |
| spa | −0.51 % | −0.88 % | −1.22 % | −1.82 % | +8.14 % | +23.46 % |
| suzuka | −0.57 % | −1.04 % | −1.49 % | −2.27 % | +7.64 % | +21.87 % |

Two metres of lateral freedom — about a fifth of a street circuit's width —
changes arc length by more than the whole deficit at every circuit. At monaco
the full observed 1.895 % corresponds to a line about 2.2 m inside the compiled
median line, and the achievable band for ±5 m of freedom spans −3.7 % to
+14.9 %. **The arc length of a driven line inside a real corridor is simply not
determined to better than a few percent**, and the sign is the expected one: a
surveyed centreline is longer than the line a driver takes.

### 6.6 Recommendation (not implemented)

**Accept a documented per-circuit tolerance with physical justification, on the
`deficit ≈ w · ∮|κ| ds` model — but do not do it by editing the three manifests
to a looser number.** Concretely, I recommend the coordinator consider:

1. Keep the 1 % declared tolerance as the *default* for driven-line packages
   (D-12 stands and monza, spa, suzuka pass it comfortably).
2. Add to D-12 a physical form for the driven-line allowance rather than a flat
   fraction: `tolerance = (w_max · ∮|κ| ds) / L_official` with a single declared
   `w_max` (2.5 m covers all six measurements, mean 1.87 m, max 2.42 m). That
   gives 0.71 % at monza, 1.06 % at spa, 1.31 % at suzuka, 2.34 % at monaco,
   1.40 % at singapore, 1.41 % at mexico-city — every circuit passes, the
   allowance is tightest where the geometry says it should be tightest, and no
   number was chosen per circuit to make a circuit pass. `∮|κ| ds` is already
   computable from the stored arrays, so the validator could derive it
   independently rather than trusting a manifest field. Note the margin is thin
   at two circuits — singapore 1.315 % against 1.40 %, mexico-city 1.367 %
   against 1.41 % — so `w_max = 2.5 m` is not a generous constant, which is the
   point; a different driver or session could still fail it, and that would be
   information rather than noise.
3. Failing that, leave monaco, mexico-city and singapore at `discovered`. That is
   an honest state: their geometry passes every integrity check and only the

## 8. Requests to the coordinator

**(a) `CompiledCentreline` needs a null elevation channel.** `package.py`
(checked again at its 2026-09-09T01:02Z revision, after another worker touched
it) rejects a non-finite value in `z_m` or `grade_rad`, so a circuit whose elevation
is unpublishable cannot say so in its arrays; today it keeps the measured
out-of-bounds values plus a flag and two notes, and relies on the validator
refusing it. What I think is needed, in your file, not mine:

* allow `z_m` / `grade_rad` to be all-`nan` when the package declares the
  channel unknown (the same treatment `width_left_m`, `width_right_m` and `mu`
  already get), with a `GeometryDescriptor` field such as
  `elevation_quality: measured | unknown` so the state is declared rather than
  inferred from `nan`;
* a validator rule to match: `no_nan` should read an unknown elevation channel
  as `unknown`, not `fail`, and `grade_bounded` as `unknown` — so the circuit
  lands at `discovered` (geometry incomplete) rather than `rejected` (integrity
  broken). Those two words describe different situations.
* downstream, `CompiledTrackSource.grade_at` returns the array value directly and
  would hand the engine a `nan`. The pattern already exists one method below:
  `mu_at` falls back to a declared `reference_mu` when the array is `nan`. An
  unknown elevation channel should get the same treatment — a *labelled*
  flat-road assumption with synthetic provenance, or an outright refusal to
  simulate — decided by you, not silently by me.

Until then the compiler behaves as documented in 4.4 and no fabricated grade
ships.

**(b) `direction` blocks a self-crossing circuit.** Suzuka's net turning is
0.000 rad, so `mixed` is correct, and the validator's `direction` check
correctly says it cannot confirm a `mixed` package — but the combination caps
suzuka at `discovered` even though every other geometry check passes. Either the
package should carry the direction of *travel* (which timing data can establish
independently of turning number) as a separate field from the turning sense, or
`_derive_status` should treat `direction: unknown` on a package that declares
`mixed` **and agrees with its manifest** as satisfied. Both are your call; I
changed neither.

**(c) Task 2, section 6.6** — the physical driven-line tolerance form for D-12.
Recommendation only; the three manifests are untouched.

## 9. Limitations, stated rather than hidden

* **No test was added for the elevation channel.** The coordinator's mid-task
  instruction was to stop writing test suites and finish the implementation, so
  the z-spike fixture test that the original brief asked for does **not** exist.
  Both branches were verified by running them (transcript in 4.4) and on real
  suzuka data, and `tests/tracks` was re-run green (127 passed), but that
  verification is not committed and will not catch a regression. If you want it
  committed, the two cases are: a synthetic lap with one absurd z sample must not
  produce an out-of-bounds smoothed grade, and an irreparable channel must be
  declared unavailable rather than fabricated.
* **The elevation profile is still unvalidated.** OpenF1's z scale is assumed
  equal to x/y — that assumption is not tested by anything here — and there is no
  survey cross-check. The published channel is only known to be *plausible*
  (bounded by a declared gradient ceiling), not correct. `geometry.elevation` and
  the package notes say so.
* **z is quantised at one coordinate unit (0.1 m).** On the two flat circuits the
  raw z channel barely moves between samples (mexico-city spans 2.8 m,
  singapore 5.3 m), so their grade is dominated by quantisation, not terrain. The
  numbers are small and bounded, but they are not a survey of a flat road.
* **The rejection test cannot see the first and last sample of an ingest
  window**, which have no two-sided neighbourhood. The window carries 1.5 s of
  margin on each side, so the lap's own timing-line neighbourhood is interior,
  but a spike exactly at a window edge would survive into the interpolated
  boundary crossing.
* **Same-sign steep segments are kept on purpose** (Monaco 1, suzuka 3). If a
  real circuit ever had a sustained gradient above 25 %, this compiler would
  keep it and then refuse to publish the channel. That is the right failure
  direction, but it means the ceiling is a publication limit, not a claim that
  nothing steeper exists.
* **Suzuka is `discovered`, not `geometry_validated`**, for the direction reason
  in section 5. The elevation defect the brief was about is fixed; this is a
  different limitation and I did not paper over it.
* **The corridor is unknown on all six**, so `mu` is not claimed, corners are not
  labelled, width arrays are `nan`, and none of these circuits can reach
  `simulation_eligible`. Unchanged, and by design.
* **Compile cost** is about 90 s per circuit on one core; the elevation selection
  adds roughly 8 s of that (measured: `process_lap` 58 s, horizontal selection
  20 s, elevation selection 7.5 s, at monza).
