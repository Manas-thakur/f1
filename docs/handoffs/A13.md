# A13 — validation, independent checking and the benchmark harness

Status: **module review-ready**. This is the *check*, not the product, and it is
not a certification of anything. Everything below was measured inside a
synthetic simulator whose every parameter is an invented assumption. No result
here describes a real car, a real circuit or a real race.

## 1. The headline result

A03's handoff says plainly that its `EnergyLedger.close_error()` is *the
simulator auditing itself* and cannot catch a bug shared between the simulator
and its checker. `packages/core/afterlap_core/evaluation/independent_ledger.py`
is the separate checker that closes that gap. It is written from the reference
equations in `simulation/NUMERICS_AND_VALIDATION.md`, and it does not import
`afterlap_core.simulation.physics` or `.battery` — a test reads the module's own
source and fails if it ever does, with a control proving the guard is not
vacuous (`tests/evaluation/test_independent_ledger.py::TestStructuralIndependence`).

### 1.1 The number

Reconstructed battery energy at the end of a 20 s run (1 000 frames, dt = 0.02 s,
both cars, all five shipped scenarios), against the simulator's own recorded
final energy. Signed as **independent − simulator**, in joules.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| two-straight-counterattack | rival | −2.9104e−09 | −9.42e−16 | +1.9907e−08 | 3.090e+06 | 983 |
| oval-low-energy | own | +1.1642e−10 | +2.69e−16 | +7.2760e−11 | 4.328e+05 | 2 |

**Worst |A13 − A03| = 3.7486e−08 J. Frozen tolerance: 1.0e−04 J.** The
reconstruction agrees with A03's ledger to fourteen significant figures relative
to the electrical energy that passed through it. **No disagreement was found.**

The two columns are deliberately not the same arithmetic, which is the point:

* A03's `close_error()` is `E_final − (E_initial + battery_in_j − battery_out_j)`,
  computed from the *terminal-side* running totals the simulator itself
  accumulated.
* A13's number goes back to the **DC-bus and auxiliary** ledgers and re-applies
  the conversions from the specification —
  `E_final = E_0 + Σ(η_charge · ΔE_harvest_dc − ΔE_deploy_dc / η_discharge − ΔE_aux)`
  — using efficiencies read from the car document.

They therefore disagree in their last digits (e.g. −3.7486e−08 against
+3.7253e−08 on the same run) because they are two different float summations of
the same physics. A shared misapplication of `η_discharge` or `η_charge` — the
error `NUMERICS_AND_VALIDATION.md` singles out — would show in A13's column and
**not** in A03's, because A03's never divides by an efficiency at that point.

### 1.2 How the tolerance was established

Per `NUMERICS_AND_VALIDATION.md` ("Establish numerical tolerances empirically
using the reference solver, then freeze them before benchmark evaluation. Do not
select a tolerance that hides a failing conservation test."):

1. Every tolerance in `LedgerTolerances` was set to `1e30`.
2. The reconstruction was run over all five scenarios, both cars, at 200 and at
   1 000 frames — twenty runs.
3. Each tolerance was then frozen above the largest residual observed, as a
   named constant with the measured number in its docstring.
4. Nothing has been widened since. Two tolerances were **tightened** after the
   first pass when it became clear the first draft had been derived from a
   whole-run total rather than a per-frame rate (see §1.4).

`battery_conversion_j = 1e-4 J` is five orders of magnitude above the observed
worst of 3.7e−08 J. That headroom is deliberate: it is the same absolute figure
A03 froze for its own `close_error()` test, so the two checks are comparable, and
it leaves room for longer runs without being anywhere near a physically
meaningful energy (1e−4 J against a 4 MJ battery is 2.5e−11 of full scale).

### 1.3 Does the check have teeth?

A tolerance that nothing can violate proves nothing, so the checker is falsified
deliberately in `TestCorruptedTrajectoryIsDetected`:

| Injected defect | Detected as |
|---|---|
| 50 kJ of battery energy appearing at frame 120 with no flow to explain it | one `unexplained_battery_energy` frame finding at exactly frame 120, residual +50 000.000000 J; `battery_energy_from_bus_ledgers` fails its tolerance with difference −50 000 J |
| CU-K recharge ledger rewritten as battery *gain* (`η_charge ×` bus energy) — the exact bus confusion `UNITS_TIME.md` forbids | `cuk_ledger_equals_dc_bus_harvest` fails; the identity `Δrecharge ≡ Δharvested_dc` is exact, so a 6 % error cannot hide in it |
| Recorded deployment halved at one frame without changing the battery state | frame finding at that frame |

The clean trajectory is asserted **not** flagged in the same test, so the
detector is not simply always-on. Across all ten headline runs, **0 of 10 000
frames** were flagged.

The sensitivity is also written out arithmetically:
`200 kW / 0.8 = 250 kW` against a wrongly-multiplied `200 kW × 0.8 = 160 kW` is
900 kJ over ten seconds — ten orders of magnitude above the tolerance.

### 1.4 What the reconstruction actually checks, and at what resolution

`reconstruct()` subdivides every recorded frame into **8** sub-intervals
(`DEFAULT_SUBSTEPS`) and integrates with classical RK4 (motion, thermal) or
composite Simpson (resistive work), against a run that used explicit midpoint
with the whole frame as its step. Ten discrepancies are produced; worst value
across the ten headline runs, each against its frozen tolerance:

| Check | Worst observed | Frozen tolerance | What it would catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| `battery_energy_from_bus_ledgers` | −3.75e−08 J | 1e−4 J | efficiency mis-application, free energy |
| `battery_energy_from_powers` | 3.06e+03 J | 1.97e+04 J (derived) | instantaneous powers inconsistent with the ledger |
| `cuk_ledger_equals_dc_bus_harvest` | 0.0 J | 1e−6 J | CU-K ledger integrating the wrong bus |
| `cuk_ledger_from_harvest_power` | 1.17e+03 J | 1.97e+04 J (derived) | harvest power inconsistent with the ledger |
| `worst_unsplit_frame_speed_residual` | 7.51e−04 m/s | 1e−2 m/s | the force balance not matching `a = (F_drive − F_drag − F_roll − F_grade)/m` |
| `worst_split_frame_speed_residual` | −2.86e−01 m/s | 2.0 m/s | as above, limited by the recording (see below) |
| `work_energy_balance_unsplit` | −1.58e+02 J | 5.0 J × unsplit frames | ΔKE not matching the work actually done |
| `work_energy_balance_split` | −1.64e+04 J | 200 J × split frames | as above, limited by the recording |
| `worst_unsplit_frame_thermal_residual` | −1.71e−13 K | 1e−9 K | `C_th dT/dt = P_loss − h(T − T_amb)` mis-integrated |
| `worst_split_frame_thermal_residual` | 1.84e−03 K | 1e−2 K | as above, limited by the recording |

The "derived" tolerances are computed per run as
`per_unsplit_frame × n_unsplit + per_split_frame × n_split`, so they scale with
the frame census rather than being a blanket number.

**Split frames are separated out for a stated reason.** A recorded frame carries
one set of instantaneous powers and forces. When the simulator split that frame
at a regulatory boundary or a delayed driver action, those values describe only
the *final* sub-step, so any check driven by them is limited by the recording and
not by the simulator. Those checks are reported separately, under their own,
explicitly looser tolerances, and the audit's `notes` say so on every run where
it applies. This is a limitation of A13's recorder, disclosed rather than
absorbed.

## 2. Findings

Five things the independent check surfaced. None is a defect in another module's
code; all five change how a result from this package should be read.

### F1 — A03's reported sub-step average does not match the shipped loop fixtures

A03's handoff states: *"Splitting stops below 1e-4 s, which bounds the number of
sub-steps; observed average is 1.00 sub-steps/step with a maximum of 2."*

Measured here, 1 000 frames at dt = 0.02 s, ego car:

| Scenario | mean sub-steps/step | max | frames with >1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| two-straight-counterattack | 1.983 | 2 | 983 / 1000 |
| loop-no-energy-channel | 1.985 | **3** | 983 / 1000 |
| loop-regen-disabled | 1.986 | **3** | 983 / 1000 |
| oval-low-energy | 1.002 | 2 | 2 / 1000 |
| oval-defend-hold | 1.001 | 2 | 1 / 1000 |

The splitting itself is correct behaviour — the step is split at the apply time
of each delayed opponent action, and every shipped scenario gives its drivers a
0.35 s reaction delay, so almost every step contains such a boundary. But the
*reported* figure of 1.00 average / max 2 holds only on the oval; on all three
`test-loop` scenarios the average is ~1.985 and the maximum is 3. Anyone sizing a
step-cost budget or a recorder from A03's figure will be out by a factor of two.

**Action for A03/coordinator:** correct the figure in `handoffs/A03.md`, or state
which fixture it was measured on.

### F2 — Seed variation is close to inert in the shipped fixtures

`simulation` has keyed RNG streams but, as A03's own limitation 7 records, no
wind or track-evolution producer exists. Sensor noise is the only thing a seed
perturbs. Measured: 200 steps of `two-straight-counterattack` at seeds 42, 43 and
99 give **bit-identical** progress (323.597666121365 m) and energy
(1 653 157.8947368653 J); only the observation differs (85.02 / 84.96 / 84.88 m/s).

Consequence for statistics: unless a controller's decision boundary happens to
fall inside the noise, different seeds produce identical trajectories, and the
seed level of the hierarchical bootstrap contributes **zero** variance. On the
smoke benchmark this is exactly what happens. Rather than emit a falsely tight
interval, `BootstrapResult` carries `degenerate_seed_variance` and
`within_scenario_variance`, and `build_report` writes a note into the contract:

> zero within-scenario seed variance for: legal_greedy_attacker. Every seed
> produced the identical paired difference, so the seed level of the
> hierarchical bootstrap contributed nothing and the interval reflects the
> scenario population alone. This is a property of the fixtures, which carry no
> exogenous physical disturbance producer, not of the estimator.

**Action:** a real study needs an exogenous disturbance producer keyed by
physical time before its five-training-seed plan means anything.

### F3 — Rival branches coincide on short horizons

A03's opponents react only through a hysteretic gap state machine, and there is
no wake model, so a rival's trajectory changes between two branches *only* once
the ego's behaviour moves the gap across a threshold. Measured on
`two-straight-counterattack` from a 4 s pre-roll snapshot, HARVEST against PUSH:

| branch horizon | rival final progress (conserve / push) | diverged? |
|---|---|---|
| 20 s | 1527.278514254518 / 1527.278514254518 | no |
| 30 s | 2330.8274 / 2330.5606 | yes |
| 40 s | 3134.4148 / 3134.2428 | yes |

The branches are structurally independent throughout (separate `Simulator`
instances, separate observations, no replay). What coincides is the *outcome*.
Both facts are asserted:
`test_different_treatments_diverge_with_the_rival_reacting` uses a 30 s horizon
and says why, and `test_the_rival_needs_a_long_enough_branch_to_diverge` pins the
20 s coincidence so it cannot drift unnoticed. `commissioning-smoke` was moved
from 8 s to 20 s for the same reason, with the reason written into the manifest.

**Consequence:** a paired comparison over a branch too short to move the
opponent's state machine measures the ego alone. Do not read it as evidence
about racecraft.

### F4 — A06's planner misses the 200 ms budget on a loaded machine, and withdraws correctly

Measured per decision on the hardware in §6:

| condition | planner duration |
|---|---|
| this acceptance file run alone | 135–170 ms |
| after the whole evaluation suite has run in the same process | 205–2 498 ms |

Against the declared 200 ms budget the planner returns
`PlanningStatus.DEADLINE_EXCEEDED` with *"the 200 ms decision budget expired
after 204.9 ms; a late plan is not published"* and publishes nothing. **That is
the specified behaviour and it worked.** It is recorded here as an observation
about this machine under load, not as a latency measurement of A06 — a real
figure needs A14's declared, quiet hardware.

The acceptance slice therefore states an ample allowance for its *functional*
tests (`MPC_BUDGET_MS = 60 000`) and tests the deadline behaviour deliberately
instead, with a 1 ms allowance
(`test_a_budget_it_cannot_meet_withdraws_the_advice`). The latency test asserts
the invariant that matters — a decision that overran its allowance must have
withdrawn — and asserts no wall-clock number.

### F5 — The held-out test manifest is a quarter of the declared population

`SERVING_AND_EVALUATION.md` sets an initial study target of at least 100
independent test scenarios stratified across families, for each of five training
seeds. `configs/benchmarks/manifests/held-out-test-v1.yaml` is frozen with the
shape the plan requires, but the package ships five synthetic scenarios across
two synthetic tracks: **25 independent units against a target of 500**. The
manifest says so in its own notes. This is a population defect and no bootstrap
interval repairs it.

The `Controller` protocol is what A06 and A07 satisfy. It takes one
`ControlRequest` and returns one `ControlDecision`:

```python
class Controller(Protocol):
    @property
    def name(self) -> str: ...
    @property
    def uses_actor(self) -> bool: ...
    @property
    def uses_learned_return(self) -> bool: ...
    def decide(self, request: ControlRequest) -> ControlDecision: ...
```

**Equal observation access is structural, not conventional.** `ControlRequest`
carries an `Observation` (the simulator's only sanctioned controller input), the
resolved `RuleContext`, the compute allowance, the freshness limit and the
exogenous disturbance stream identifiers. It carries no simulator, no
`WorldState` and no rival truth. `assert_no_truth_access()` inspects the
dataclass definition and fails if a field named `world`, `simulator`, `truth`,
`world_state`, `rival_truth` or `ledger` is ever added — before anyone can read
from it.

A06's planner satisfies the protocol through the adapter in
`tests/acceptance/conftest.py::MpcController`, which lives in the test suite
rather than in `evaluation/` deliberately: **no module in `evaluation/` imports
`afterlap_core.planning` or `afterlap_core.estimation`**, and a test asserts it
over every file in the package.

## 6. Statistics: what is enforced

* **Hierarchical paired bootstrap.** Scenarios are resampled with replacement,
  then seeds within each drawn scenario. Both arms of a comparison move together
  because a unit carries every controller's value for the same episode; there is
  no code path that resamples one controller independently of another.
* **Correlated telemetry frames cannot be resampled as trials.** The resampler
  accepts a validated `PairedSample` only. A bare sequence, or a sequence of
  `TelemetryFrameSeries`, raises `CorrelatedSampleError` with a message naming
  the mistake. Repeating one `(scenario, seed)` across many units — the shape a
  caller reaches for when inflating a sample size — is refused at construction.
  Frames enter only through `EvaluationUnit.from_frame_series(..., reducer=...)`,
  which forces the reduction to be stated, and which refuses frames belonging to
  a different episode.
* **The interval always contains its point estimate.** A percentile interval that
  excluded it would be rejected by the `BenchmarkComparison` contract; it is
  widened to contain it (the conservative direction) and
  `interval_widened_to_contain_estimate` records that it happened.
* **Distributions, not just means.** `summarise` reports mean, sd, min, q05,
  median, q95, max, the CVaR of the worst `1 − alpha` fraction oriented by the
  metric's declared direction, and the named worst cases.
* **Calibration refuses to score what it cannot.** A set with only positive
  labels, only negative labels, no samples, or support below the declared
  minimum returns `CalibrationStatus.UNAVAILABLE` with its support count and a
  stated reason, and **no** Brier score and **no** log loss. A scorable set is
  still only `CALIBRATED` if the largest predicted-versus-observed gap across
  populated bins is within the declared limit; otherwise the scores are reported
  under `UNCALIBRATED`, which is a result rather than a refusal.

**On A05's intervals.** A05's handoff records rival energy interval coverage at
0.7885 against a nominal 0.90 label. Nothing in A13 consumes that interval as a
bound: the acceptance adapter passes the contract fixture's rival belief through
with its `kind="quantile"`, `coverage=0.9` label intact, and the adapter's
docstring states that it is a model quantile and not a validated bound. No
calibration report in this package is fitted to or scored against it. If a future
report does score it, it must be scored, not asserted.

### Line-boundary timing, against the independent reference

The independent reference locates each crossing by cubic-Hermite bisection on the
recorded progress trace; the simulator splits its step at a linearly interpolated
crossing. Measured agreement on `two-straight-counterattack`:

| step | reference crossings | recorded crossings | worst difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| dt = 0.02 s | 1 | 1 | 3.62e−12 s |
| dt = 0.05 s | 1 | 1 | −6.53e−10 s |

The error grows with the step, as expected, and both are far inside the frozen
1e−8 s tolerance. The reference is separately validated against an analytically
known constant-acceleration crossing
(`t = (−v₀ + √(v₀² + 2a(P − p₀)))/a`, recovered to 1e−9 s) and shown to beat
linear interpolation on the same samples by more than a factor of 100.

## 10. Known limitations

1. **Not a fidelity claim.** Everything checked here is the simulator's
   self-consistency against its own stated equations. No parameter is measured,
   no real-car data exists in this package, and no result supports a claim about
   a real vehicle.
2. **The recorder's resolution bounds three checks.** A recorded frame carries
   one set of instantaneous powers and forces; where the simulator split the
   frame, those describe only the final sub-step. On the `test-loop` fixtures
   that is 983 frames in 1 000 (F1), and the motion, work and thermal checks are
   correspondingly weak there. The battery ledger check is unaffected because it
   uses the exact per-frame ledger deltas. Fixing this properly needs a
   sub-step-resolved hook from A03.
3. **Grade is never exercised by a fixture.** Both shipped tracks are level at
   every breakpoint, so `F_grade = m g sin(grade)` contributes nothing to any
   recorded trajectory. It is covered only by a hand-computed unit test, and
   every audit note says so for the track it ran on.
4. **The MPC row is not yet a benchmark row.** It runs in the acceptance slice
   through a synthetic pass-through adapter, not through A05's estimator. No
   benchmark number is produced for it and the report says `unmeasured`.
5. **No probability calibration is measured.** No component in this package emits
   a probabilistic event forecast yet, so `assess_calibration` is exercised only
   against synthetic forecasts in the unit tests. The report says calibration is
   unmeasured rather than implying it is perfect.
6. **Withdrawal accounting is per decision tick, not per published
   recommendation.** The lifecycle that turns a decision into a published,
   expiring recommendation is A08's; when it exists the denominators should be
   re-derived from it.
7. **`losing_snapshots` archives identifiers, not bytes.** It records the
   scenario, seed, manifest hash and snapshot hash, which is enough to restore
   the exact starting state, but it does not store the snapshot itself. A
   reproduction therefore needs the same bundle present.
8. **Statistical power is not claimed anywhere.** The smoke manifest has four
   units; the held-out manifest has 25 against a target of 500 (F5). Neither can
   support a promotion decision and both say so in their own notes.
9. **Cross-platform reproducibility is not claimed**, following A03's scoping.

## 12. Fixture provenance and source notes

Both benchmark manifests are `synthetic: true` and were written by hand for this
package. They reference only A03's five shipped scenarios and A04's shipped rule
packs; they invent no scenario and no parameter. Their `frozen_at` timestamps are
declared, and their notes record the gap between the declared study target and
the shipped population rather than hiding it.

Every number in `LedgerTolerances` was measured on those fixtures by the
procedure in §1.2 and is carried as a named constant with its measurement in the
docstring. The reference equations were transcribed from
`simulation/NUMERICS_AND_VALIDATION.md`; the track interpolation was
re-implemented from the scheme described in A03's `TrackSegment` docstring and is
cross-checked against A03's own interpolator in
`test_the_track_reference_is_not_the_simulators_interpolator` — agreement is the
expected result there, and the point is that it is checked rather than assumed.

No third-party code was copied. No data of any kind was imported from outside
this workspace. The dependencies used are the ones the coordinator already locked
(numpy, scipy, pydantic, PyYAML).
