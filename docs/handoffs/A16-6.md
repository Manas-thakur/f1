## Functional forms and every coefficient

### Wake (`wake.py`)

One dimensionless *shielding* fraction drives both multipliers:

```text
decay(x)   = (exp(-x/L) - exp(-R/L)) / (1 - exp(-R/L))   for 0 <= x <= R,  else 0
falloff(y) = exp(-(y/W)^2)      when a corridor width exists
           = 1.0                when there is no corridor (in-line assumption)
gate(v)    = min(1, v / V_ref)
shielding  = decay(x) * falloff(y) * gate(v_leader)

drag_multiplier      = 1 - D_max * shielding
downforce_multiplier = 1 - F_max * shielding
```

`x` is nose-to-tail longitudinal separation, `y` the lateral centre offset,
`v_leader` the leader's ground speed. `decay` is a **range-truncated normalised
exponential**: exactly `1.0` at zero separation and exactly `0.0` at `R`, so the
effect switches off continuously and a car with nothing inside `R` gets
multipliers that are exactly `1.0`.

| Symbol | Constant | Value | Unit | Verification | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `R` | `WAKE_RANGE_M` | 40.0 | m | `synthetic_assumption` | Declared range; beyond it, free air. Order-of-magnitude wake length for a 5–6 m car, not a measured extent. |
| `L` | `WAKE_DECAY_LENGTH_M` | 12.0 | m | `synthetic_assumption` | e-folding length of the momentum deficit before the range normalisation. |
| `D_max` | `WAKE_DRAG_REDUCTION_MAX` | 0.28 | 1 | `synthetic_assumption` | CdA fraction removed at zero separation, in line, above `V_ref`. The single most influential uncalibrated coefficient here. |
| `F_max` | `WAKE_DOWNFORCE_LOSS_MAX` | 0.35 | 1 | `synthetic_assumption` | ClA fraction lost under the same conditions. Larger than `D_max` so following costs grip as well as saving drag; the ratio is an assumption. |
| `W` | `WAKE_LATERAL_SCALE_M` | 1.60 | m | `synthetic_assumption` | Gaussian half-width of the lateral falloff. Evaluated **only** when a corridor width exists. |
| `V_ref` | `WAKE_SPEED_REFERENCE_MPS` | 30.0 | m/s | `synthetic_assumption` | Leader speed at which the deficit is at full strength; below it the shielding ramps linearly to zero, so a stationary leader casts no wake. |

Source label on all six: `synthetic:afterlap-wake-v1`.

Effect labels: `free_air`, `wake:disabled` (no model installed),
`wake:lateral_resolved`, `wake:unknown_corridor_inline_assumption`.

Deliberately **not** modelled: yaw (the spec lists it; resolving a yawed wake
needs lateral geometry this model may not assume), and any dependence of the
multipliers on relative speed (accepted and recorded, but no rate law is
asserted without evidence).

### Overtake stages (`overtake.py`)

| Symbol | Constant | Value | Unit | Verification | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| — | `CLOSE_SEPARATION_M` | 15.0 | m | `synthetic_assumption` | Nose-to-tail separation at or below which the follower has *closed*. A staging threshold for metrics, not a physical limit. |
| — | `CLOSE_RELEASE_M` | 25.0 | m | `synthetic_assumption` | Separation beyond which a new attempt re-arms. The gap to 15.0 m is the hysteresis. |

Source label: `synthetic:afterlap-overtake-v1`.

Stage rules, all from `WorldState` truth (`clear` = half the sum of the two car
lengths, the same definition the engine's pass detector uses):

* `close` — `separation <= CLOSE_SEPARATION_M` while still behind. Latches;
  re-arms only past `CLOSE_RELEASE_M` and only if no pass completed.
* `overlap` — along-track extents overlap (`|delta| < clear`) **and** the
  footprints do not intersect, i.e. genuinely side by side with space. Needs a
  corridor.
* `pass_complete` — `delta > clear` in unwrapped progress. When a corridor
  exists, interlocked footprints veto it (matching the engine's
  `blocked_by_contact` rule); with no corridor the veto cannot be evaluated and
  the stage is credited from longitudinal progress alone.
* `retained` — re-checked at the named checkpoint. Latching `pass_complete`
  plus `not_reached` here is exactly how a lost position is recorded.

Statuses: `reached`, `not_reached`, `pending` (decidable, deciding event not yet
happened), `unavailable` (the reduced model cannot decide; carries
`unavailable_reason`).

### Policy interaction memory (`policies.py`)

| Constant | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `RESPONSE_BIN_S` | 0.25 s | Physical time bin over which the drawn response bias is held constant. |
| `RESPONSE_JITTER_FRACTION` | 0.05 | Fractional perturbation of the engage threshold per standard deviation. |
| `RESPONSE_JITTER_CLIP` | 2.0 | Bias clip, so the threshold moves by at most ±10%. |
| `INTERACTION_HISTORY_STEPS` | 12 | Retained gap history length; bounded so snapshots stay small. |
| `PRESSURE_COMMIT_STEPS` | 5 | Consecutive steps under pressure before a conserving driver reacts. |
| `ATTEMPT_COOLDOWN_STEPS` | 25 | Steps an attacker cools after an attempt fizzles. |
| `RECOVERY_STEPS` | 40 | Steps a driver stays in its post-pass response after losing a place. |

These are module-level tuning constants, **not** in `policy_hash` (see
"Coordinator requests").

Memory (JSON, captured and restored with the snapshot, defaults re-applied on
restore so an older snapshot cannot KeyError): `state`, `steps`, `bias_bin`,
`response_bias`, `pressure_steps`, `tow_steps`, `passed_by`, `passes_made`,
`attempts`, `cooldown`, `recovery`, `ahead_id`, `behind_id`, `gap_ahead_s`,
`gap_behind_s`, `closing_rate_mps`, bounded `history`.

New reactions, each additive and each inert when no interaction is present:

* `normal` — new `tow_straight` state: in a tow on a straight it deploys `PUSH`
  instead of `NEUTRAL`.
* `conserve` — new `pressured` state: sustained pressure for
  `PRESSURE_COMMIT_STEPS` steps suspends the saving while the margin allows it.
* `attack` — new `cooling` state: an attempt that reached `committed` and then
  lost the gap counts as a failed attempt and cools for
  `ATTEMPT_COOLDOWN_STEPS`. Completing the pass clears the cooldown.
* `defend` — new `recovering` state: once actually passed it deploys and takes
  the mirrored line back for `RECOVERY_STEPS`, instead of returning to `clear`
  and cruising.

Randomness: exactly one draw per `react`, taken **before** the observation is
inspected, from the engine's existing named stream `driver_response:<car_id>`
(`StreamRegistry`, state captured in the snapshot). Count-stable by
construction, so two branches taking the same number of steps consume the
stream identically; the drawn value is only *applied* once per
`RESPONSE_BIN_S` physical bin, so branches reaching the same instant apply the
same perturbation. The perturbed engage threshold is clamped below the release
gap, so the hysteresis ordering the constructor validates still holds, and
`pace_scale` bounds are untouched — the bias is a timing preference, never a
licence to exceed the envelope.

## Engine wiring (what is live in a run)

The aero change is two multiplications at the single existing site in
`_evaluate`:

```python
wake_effect = self._wake_effect_for(car_id, progress_m, lateral_d_m, speed)
self._wake_effects[car_id] = wake_effect
down_n = physics.downforce(rho, wake_effect.downforce_multiplier * float(car.cla_m2.value), air_speed)
...
drag_n = physics.drag_force(rho, wake_effect.drag_multiplier * float(car.cda_m2.value), air_speed)
```

`_wake_effect_for` picks the leader as the nearest car ahead in unwrapped
progress from `WorldState` (truth, not an observation), reads the leader's
state at the start of the sub-step, and passes a lateral offset **only** when
`width_at` is finite at both cars' positions — the same condition the engine
already uses to enable the lateral degree of freedom.

`_commit` mirrors the existing `electrical_limits` pattern to publish
`report.wake_effects[car_id] = effect.describe()`. `CarState` has
`slots=True`, so no new diagnostic field was added there and no snapshot schema
changed.

`Simulator.follow_overtake(a, b)` registers a pair; `step` feeds every
registered tracker after pass detection and `_evaluate_retention` decides
`retained` at the scenario's retention checkpoint. Nothing is registered by
default, so `report.overtake_stages` is empty and a run that does not ask for
stage labelling behaves exactly as before.

**Wake is opt-in.** `reset(..., wake=None)` is the default because every
coefficient is an uncalibrated assumption; a run has to ask for it. With no
model the multipliers are `WakeEffect(1.0, 1.0)` and `1.0 * x == x` exactly in
IEEE-754, so the trajectory is bit-identical to the pre-wake engine.

## What is refused on a corridor-less circuit, and why

Decision D-10 and `RACE_CONDITION_MODEL.md`: "Longitudinal gap alone can
estimate a tow but cannot establish side-by-side space or contact probability."
A compiled driven-line package (`monza`, `openf1_location_telemetry`
provenance) has `lateral_geometry_surveyed is False` and `width_at` returns
`nan`.

Refused (returned as `unavailable` with `unavailable_reason="corridor_unknown"`
and a detail naming the condition, never as `not_reached`):

* the `overlap` stage — side-by-side space is not established;
* `ContactAssessment` — the footprint test needs a lateral position that means
  something;
* any lateral conclusion from a `WakeEffect`: `lateral_known` is `False` and
  `describe()["lateral_claims"]` reads
  `"refused: no corridor at this position"`.

Still available, because they are decidable from unwrapped longitudinal
progress alone: `close`, `pass_complete`, `retained`, and the tow itself — which
is computed under the explicit in-line assumption and labelled
`wake:unknown_corridor_inline_assumption`. The `overlap` refusal detail also
reports how many steps had overlapping along-track extents and could not be
resolved either way, so the refusal is not silent about what was lost.

One deliberate distinction: `corridor_known` (a finite width exists) is
separate from `corridor_surveyed` (`lateral_geometry_surveyed`). A synthetic
track document declares a width with `synthetic_assumption` provenance, the
engine already permits lateral motion and runs its footprint test there, and so
does this module — while every record carries
`lateral_geometry_surveyed=False`, so a decidable `overlap` on a synthetic
corridor can never be read as a measured one. Only an absent corridor (`nan`)
triggers the refusal. Making the tracker stricter than the engine would have
left the two labellings disagreeing on the shipped synthetic scenarios.

## What remains uncalibrated

* All six wake coefficients and both overtake staging thresholds. Per the
  calibration hierarchy in `RACE_CONDITION_MODEL.md`, traffic interaction is
  calibrated *after* geometry, free-air pace, braking, the energy ledger and
  thermal response; none of that has produced a residual set for traffic yet, so
  the numbers are order-of-magnitude assumptions chosen for plausible dynamics.
* The `D_max : F_max` ratio (0.28 : 0.35) encodes "following costs more grip
  than it saves drag". That direction is a modelling choice, not a measurement.
* The seven policy reactivity constants. They produce visibly different
  opponents, which is the behavioural requirement; no claim is made that any of
  them matches a real driver's response timing.
* `RESPONSE_JITTER_FRACTION` gives a ±10 % engage-threshold spread. That is a
  bounded timing preference, not a fitted distribution of human variability.
* Nothing here contributes to D-06's missing *physical* exogenous disturbance:
  the response bias reaches a policy's threshold, not the equations of motion,
  so it does not restore seed-level variance to the bootstrap.

   *not* in `policy_hash`, deliberately, so that snapshots and any pinned
   policy identities keep restoring. That means two code versions with different
   constants share a hash. At the next contract revision, please fold a
   `reactive_layer` version tag into the hash payload.
6. **Keyed RNG for policies.** `OpponentPolicy.react(observation, rng)` receives
   a `StreamRegistry` generator, so a policy cannot key a draw by physical time
   directly; I approximated it by drawing unconditionally and holding the value
   across a `RESPONSE_BIN_S` bin. Passing the world's `KeyedRandom` (or a
   pre-keyed generator) into `react` would make the physical-time keying
   structural rather than a convention. That is a contract change and needs your
   sign-off.
7. **The `artifacts/tracks/` mutation above (defect 2).** Worth a decision:
   either the track tests get their own temporary `Paths` throughout (as
   `tests/tracks/conftest.py` already does for its synthetic packages), or the
   recompile step re-validates before it finishes.
