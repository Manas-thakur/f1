## 3. Design decisions worth reviewing

### 3.1 The observation path, and why the bridge exists

The encoder needs a `StateEstimate`. A05's estimator consumes `TelemetryEvent`
values. `bridge.ObservationBridge` performs exactly the shape conversion between
`Simulator.observe()` and `estimation.update()` and nothing else — it holds no
reference to the simulator, never reads `WorldState`, and raises
`TruthLeakError` if a rival record ever presents an energy or thermal channel.

It does **not** re-implement A02's ingestion pipeline. Deduplication,
backpressure and quality tracking belong to that pipeline and are absent here.
An integration that wants those must route through
`afterlap_api.session.runtime`; this is an in-process training loop.

### 3.2 The declared warm-up — read this one

The shipped rule pack resolves the overtake permission only at its detection
line (`s_m = 1600`). Until it is crossed, `check_plan` returns `unknown` for
`overtake_eligibility`, `unknown` is not acceptance, and **every** candidate is
rejected. A scenario whose ego starts at 0 m therefore spends its first ~23
seconds with the planner accepting nothing and advice withdrawn on every tick.

Measured before the fix, on `two-straight-counterattack` with a constant action:
36 of 36 decisions `no_feasible_candidate`, all with
`overtake_eligibility = unknown`.

`env-v1.yaml` therefore declares a `warmup_to_progress_m` per scenario. The
environment integrates to that progress under a declared conserving profile
before the first scored tick, running the permission machine over the warm-up so
it sees the crossing. The warm-up is part of the **scenario starting state**: it
is identical for every controller, it is reported in the reset info
(`warmup_s`, `start_progress_m`, `eligibility`), and nothing is scored during
it. `tests/learning/test_env.py::TestEligibility` asserts the permission is
resolved at the first scored tick and says in its own failure message why that
matters.

### 3.3 Planner mode is part of the environment revision

`planner_mode` is one of `full`, `no_rollout` or `disabled` and it appears in
`environment_version`, which is written into every checkpoint and every bundle.
A replay buffer collected under one mode is refused by `restore_into` under
another. The shipped default is `no_rollout` and §6 gives the measured reason.

### 3.4 Terminal states

* **Finish** — the ego completes the scenario's declared `race_distance_m`.
  `terminated=True`, finish position recorded, potential zeroed, the partial
  last physics interval charged its actual elapsed time.
* **Physical failure** — the simulator records an `envelope_exceedance`, i.e. a
  state its reduced model cannot support. `terminated=True`, `-1200`.
* **Truncation** — `max_episode_steps` reached. `truncated=True`, terminal
  observation preserved, potential **retained**.
* A software error is not a policy DNF: it propagates, and the episode is
  excluded from learning transitions rather than scored as a cheap terminal.

### 3.5 What the reward cannot do

`step_reward` has no pass argument and no energy argument. A pass bonus and a
finish-energy bonus are not merely unset, there is nowhere for them to enter.
The failure penalty is re-derived against the discounted running-cost bound by
`RewardManifest` at load time, and `assert_field_size_supported` refuses a
scenario family with more cars than the bound assumed.

### 3.6 The continuation adapter's approximation, stated plainly

The planner hands `ContinuationModel` a seven-field mapping describing the
terminal belief at the end of its explicit horizon. The ensemble takes a
192-value encoding. `PlannerContinuationAdapter` builds the terminal encoding as
*the decision-time encoding with the rolled-out terminal energy substituted*.
Fields the rollout does not predict are carried forward from the decision-time
estimate rather than invented, and the result still has to clear the frozen
support gate. **This is an approximation and it is recorded as one.** A caller
that wants an exact terminal encoding must encode a terminal `StateEstimate` and
call `ContinuationEnsemble.score` directly.

**What this establishes.**

* **Every episode completed legally.** 100 finishes, 0 physical failures, 0
  truncations, and **zero** decisions where the published head profile was
  outside the resolved `admissible_profiles`.
* **Energy ledgers close.** Worst `EnergyLedger.close_error()` across all 100
  episodes is 3.7e-09 J against a 4 MJ battery. That is A03's own check, not an
  independent one — A13 owns the independent ledger — but it confirms nothing in
  this module's stepping breaks the simulator's conservation.
* **Determinism and seed behaviour, together.** Each scenario shows exactly 5
  distinct returns over 25 episodes: the 5 repeats of a seed reproduce
  bit-for-bit, and the 5 seeds differ from each other. That is exactly D-06's
  picture — seeds move sensor noise, delay and quantisation, so they move the
  controller's decisions and hence the return, while the underlying physics is
  unchanged.
* **Driver delay is exercised.** Every scenario declares
  `reaction_delay_mean_s = 0.35 s`, and instructions pass through the
  simulator's reaction-delay queue rather than taking effect within their own
  tick.

**And what it exposes, which matters more.** Two of the four families produce a
**100 % withdrawal rate under the reference controller**, and a third produces
62 %. `oval-low-energy` sits too close to the energy floor for a candidate to
converge; `loop-no-energy-channel` has no observable stored energy, so the
planner correctly returns `input_unavailable` at every tick. Both behaviours are
*specified* — and both mean those families give an actor almost no decision to
learn from. Only `two-straight-counterattack` offers a usable training signal as
things stand (81 % of its decisions accepted). **A curriculum built on these four
families as they are would spend most of its transitions on ticks where the
action is inert.** That is a scenario-design finding for the coordinator, and it
is recorded here rather than smoothed over.

## 6. Measured throughput, and what it rules out

`SAC_IMPLEMENTATION.md` requires at least 1 000 transitions to be benchmarked
before a total step count is chosen. `benchmark_throughput` does that with a
uniform random policy and no gradient work, and it reports step cost and reset
cost separately because the declared warm-up (§3.2) is scenario setup, not a
scored transition. Raw output:
`artifacts/learning-measurements/throughput.json`.

| Configuration | Transitions | Wall clock (s) | End-to-end tps | Episodes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `no_rollout`, `two-straight-counterattack` | 1000 | 207.0 | **4.83** | 28 |
| `full`, `two-straight-counterattack` | 200 | 99.4 | **2.01** | 5 |
| `no_rollout`, mixed (shipped default) | 1000 | 161.5 | **6.19** | 31 |
| reference run, mixed, real controller | 3175 | 570.2 | **5.57** | 100 |

Hardware: the laptop named in §4, under contention. Treat these as floors.

### 6.1 `planner_mode: full` withdraws every decision on this host

In the `full` measurement, **15 of 15 solves returned `deadline_exceeded`** and
therefore withdrew — `solver_timeouts: 15`, `withdrawals: 15`,
`observed_executions: 0`. The full path re-simulates finalists, which A06
measured at p50 = 159 ms / p95 = 167 ms on an unloaded host and 827 ms p95 over
a sustained burst, against the declared 200 ms budget.

That is the *specified* behaviour — a late answer is not an answer — but its
consequence for learning is decisive: **under `full`, the environment produces
transitions in which the action never reaches the car.** Training on those
transitions would teach a critic about an action that had no effect. The shipped
`env-v1.yaml` therefore sets `planner_mode: no_rollout`, and that choice is
written into `environment_version` so it can never be silently compared against
a `full` run.

### 6.2 What the throughput rules out

At the shipped configuration, ~5–6 transitions/second:

| Target | Transitions | Wall clock at 5.5 tps |
|---|---|---|
| smoke job (`SAC_IMPLEMENTATION` step 2) | 10 000 | ~30 min |
| one training seed at the `sac-v1.yaml` placeholder | 200 000 | ~10 hours |
| five seeds at that placeholder | 1 000 000 | ~50 hours |
| the specification's illustrative million-step run, per seed | 1 000 000 | ~50 hours |

Vectorising four environments could help, but the cost here is a CasADi solve
plus a full-physics simulator step, both CPU-bound, on a machine already
contended — and no measurement of `SubprocVecEnv` was taken, so no speedup is
claimed. The `total_timesteps: 200000` in `sac-v1.yaml` is explicitly labelled a
placeholder to be replaced from a measurement, and this is that measurement.

## 10. Known limitations and remaining defects

### 10.1 Learning is incomplete

No SAC training run of a size that could produce a policy was executed, and no
held-out benchmark evaluation exists. §7 gives the measured throughput that
makes this a resource statement rather than a design one, and the resumable
commands. **Nothing in this module may be described as a trained model.**

### 10.2 The support thresholds are placeholders

`configs/learning/value-v1.yaml` sets `frozen_before_final_test: false` and says
so in its own comment. The three gates were chosen to be conservative — they
disable learned scoring readily — not derived from a calibration set. A constant
chosen after seeing a loss is not a deployment gate, and these are not one
either. Deriving them requires calibration episodes that do not yet exist.

### 10.3 No probability calibrator

`calibrator.json` in every bundle written today says `status: unavailable`. Pass
and retained-position probabilities need independent calibration episodes; with
none, the honest report is "unavailable", not a precise band.

### 10.4 `planner_mode: full` cannot meet its own deadline on this host

Measured: every decision in `full` mode returned `deadline_exceeded` inside the
200 ms budget and withdrew. That is the *correct* behaviour — a late answer is
not an answer — but it means the shipped training configuration runs
`no_rollout`, which trades the re-simulation evidence for throughput. The trade
is recorded in `environment_version` so a bundle trained under it cannot be
silently compared against one trained under `full`.

### 10.5 The bridge is not A02's ingestion pipeline

`bridge.ObservationBridge` performs the shape conversion only. Deduplication,
backpressure, integration-gap tracking and quality events are absent. A learner
run against a real feed must go through `afterlap_api.session.runtime` instead.

### 10.6 D-06 bounds what any later evaluation can claim

There is no exogenous physical disturbance in the simulator, so seeds drive
sensor noise, delay and quantisation but not physics. A held-out interval built
from these scenarios is scenario-resampled only and must be described that way.
This module inherits that limitation; it does not fix it and does not work
around it.

### 10.7 The continuation adapter's terminal encoding is approximate

See §3.6. The known-mask fraction of the substituted encoding still has to clear
the frozen support gate, but the carried-forward fields are decision-time values
rather than rolled-out ones.

### 10.8 `oval-low-energy` produces almost no learnable signal

Its ego starts too close to the energy floor for the planner to accept a
candidate, so most of its ticks withdraw. It is retained deliberately — the
specification asks for low-energy failures in the training data and the family
exercises the disabled-capability path — but it is not a scenario an actor can
learn energy allocation from.

### 10.9 `loop-no-energy-channel` disables the learned path entirely

By construction: with no observable stored energy the action bounds are
unusable, `learned_enabled` is false at every tick, and the planner returns
`input_unavailable`. That is the specified behaviour under a missing energy
capability and the scenario exists to exercise it.

### 10.10 Vectorised training is untested at scale

`build_vec_env` supports `SubprocVecEnv` but the default is `DummyVecEnv`. On a
laptop where a CasADi solve dominates, oversubscribing BLAS threads across
workers is as likely to hurt as help, and no measurement was taken either way.
